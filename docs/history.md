# How the field reached this point

This is a selective chronology, not a priority adjudication. Publication year is taken from the journal issue where available; online-first dates sometimes precede it.

## The remembered San Antonio paper

[Xiong, Fox & Gao (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.10124) reported visuomotor activations with about 1% magnitude contrast, 100 ms sampling and 3 mm spatial resolution, attributed to neuronal magnetic fields. This is almost certainly the paper matching the institution and approximate date in the question. Its claim should be read alongside subsequent negative experiments, rather than treated as an established imaging capability.

There were predecessors: [Kamei et al. (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1109/20.800771) reported neuronal-current distribution imaging, and [Bodurka & Bandettini (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.10159) demonstrated transient-field detection in a wire phantom. Thus “the first paper” depends on whether one means a proposal, phantom experiment, or claimed human observation. The 1999 record was checked through the original authors' later account and the Xiong paper's references; its full original text was not inspected here.

## Bandettini's connection

Three especially useful starting points are:

1. **Bodurka & Bandettini, 2002.** Spin-echo timing selected brief field changes while suppressing slowly changing fields; the phantom experiment reported detection of a 200 pT field lasting 40 ms. This is a measurement demonstration, not proof of human neural-current detection. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.10159)
2. **Bandettini, Petridou & Bodurka, 2005.** “Direct detection of neuronal activity with MRI: Fantasy, possibility, or reality?” captures the debate at the time. [NIMH publication record](https://fim.nimh.nih.gov/publications/bandettini_et_al_2005_0/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03166956)
3. **Petridou et al., 2006.** Organotypic rat-brain cultures removed the vascular system from the experiment. MR changes were compared with extracellular recordings and tetrodotoxin blockade, with spectroscopy at 7 T and imaging at 3 T. This is important biological evidence under controlled conditions, with limited transfer to the intact human brain. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0603219103)

Bandettini also coauthored the 2024 DIANA nonreplication led by Choi and colleagues. The connection therefore extends into the recent debate. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adl0999)

## Selected milestones

| Period | Development | What to retain |
|---|---|---|
| 1999–2003 | Kamei; Bodurka–Bandettini; Xiong–Fox–Gao | Distinguish phantom sensitivity from human attribution. |
| 2004 | Chu et al., visual-response experiment | No convincing rapid neuronal-current signal under the tested conditions. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2004.07.003) |
| 2006 | Petridou cultures; Truong–Song NAMO | Biological preparations and Lorentz-effect encoding pursued different routes. [NAMO](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0605486103) |
| 2008 | Cassarà et al., realistic neuronal simulations | Morphology, synchrony, source orientation and voxel size materially affect predictions. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.08.048) |
| 2008–2010 | POSSUM current-timing simulations | Acquisition timing changes the spatial appearance of current effects. [2008 abstract](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/08MProceedings/PDFfiles/00405.pdf), [2010 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mri.2010.03.029) |
| 2009 | Human-leg MREIT | Conductivity imaging with externally injected currents reaches human experiments. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2009.2018112) |
| 2016 | Sundaram et al., isolated intact turtle cerebellum | Positive phase/current reconstruction with concurrent LFP; the preparation is in vitro and pharmacologically modified. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.01.059) |
| 2017–2019 | Functional MREIT and active bidomain modeling | Activity-dependent conductivity offers another mechanism. [2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.08.004), [2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.27351) |
| 2018 | Human-brain MRCDI and DT-MREIT | Direct relevance to stimulation-field validation and conductivity estimation. [MRCDI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.12.075), [DT-MREIT](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2017.2783348) |
| 2019 | Truong et al., NEMO | Spin-lock human alpha-band report, with phantom and EEG work. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.27654) |
| 2020–2021 | ULF SIRS and 3D dcNCI phantoms | Alternative encoding regimes, still requiring sensitivity gains and biological validation. [SIRS](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/ab87fe), [dcNCI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphy.2021.647376) |
| 2022–2025 | DIANA claim, replication tests, retraction | Preserve the complete publication history. [Original, retracted](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36227975/), [notice](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40997196/) |
| 2023 | TRIGGER preprint | Claimed rapid human responses; no independent confirmation identified here. [Preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.05.31.542967) |
| 2024–2026 | Spin-lock refinements and negative human benchmark | Sequence development continues; human neural specificity remains unresolved. [Pilot](https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.5280), [2026 benchmark](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.70624) |
| 2026 | qsMRI and follow-up mini-review | Both are preprints from the proposing research line. [Research preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16423v2), [mini-review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21106v2) |

The recurring historical lesson is that a plausible neural latency, an activation-shaped map, or a successful current phantom does not alone identify the source of an in vivo MR signal. The simulation program should make those competing explanations testable.
